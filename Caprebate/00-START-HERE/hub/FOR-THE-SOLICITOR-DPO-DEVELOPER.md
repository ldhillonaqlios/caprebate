## The honest position, and it is different from the other two registers

 There are two companion registers, FOR THE ACCOUNTANT and FOR THE SURVEYOR. Both open with the same sentence: _we built everything and we switched nothing on._ **This register cannot say that, and it would be dishonest to copy it.**

 **Some of what is here is already in force.** The audit log behaves the way it behaves today. The client's uploaded file is kept today. The register of personal data breaches sits where it sits, with the access rules it has, today. The back end holds what the specification says it holds, or it will the moment it is built.

 **So what is being asked for here is mostly confirmation of a live position, not permission to take one.** That is the whole difference between this register and the other two, and it changes how urgent each entry is:

-  Where the tax adviser or the surveyor says "no", a switch stays off and **nothing changes**.

-  Where the people in this document say "no", **something that is running has to stop or be rebuilt** — and in one case the thing that has to stop is the business.

 **Two entries are the exception and are marked as such:** the retention period on a client's uploaded document is genuinely inert — the switch behind it is off, and while it is off nothing computes a deletion date at all — and the impact assessment on the document pipeline gates two document types that have not been built.

 **No claim has been filed, no client has been billed, and no client document has ever been uploaded.** Caprebate is pre-launch. That is what makes almost all of this cheap to fix if an answer goes against us, and it will not stay true for long.

## Added 8 September 2026 — staff can now put a photograph on their own account, and it has no retention rule

 **Phase 105.** Nothing in this register changed state and nothing new awaits your _signature_. What is here is **one new question for the data protection officer**, written up in full in CAPREBATE QUESTIONS under the title **"How long do we keep a member of staff's photograph after they leave?"**, plus a short note on the controls around it so that the question can be answered without reading the code.

### What was built, in the terms you will ask about it

 A member of staff can upload a photograph of themselves on the settings screen. It is personal data of the most recognisable kind, and it is the sort that goes missing from a retention schedule because it does not live in anything anybody would search for as a "personal data table".

 **Where it is.** Not in the database. It goes into the same hardened store that holds client documents: **outside the web root, directory owner-traverse only, file owner-read-write with no execute bit for anybody**, and it is served only through an application endpoint that authorises that specific request. The object key is a hash of the user id, so a listing of the storage directory is not also a list of who has an account.

 **What is stored is not what was sent.** The uploaded file is decoded to pixels and a **new** file is written from those pixels. Every text chunk, colour profile, embedded metadata block and anything appended after the end of the image is gone by construction rather than by being stripped — there is no list of things to remove and therefore no list to get out of date. The allow-list is PNG alone, because this build re-encodes what it can decode honestly and will not store a file it has not rebuilt. There are tests that build a real image with a payload hidden in it and assert the payload is not in what we keep.

 **Who may do it.** Only the person themselves — including against the super user, deliberately. An administrator can create an account, change its role and disable it; an administrator cannot change somebody's name, username or face. Editing those produces a record that reads as the person themselves, and no amount of privilege makes that something other than impersonation.

 **Deletion works, and is proved rather than assumed.** A person can remove their own photograph and the file is deleted from the store; the store re-reads the key afterwards and fails if anything survived, because evidence of a deletion that did not happen is worse than no evidence.

### The gap, which is the question

 **Disabling an account does nothing to the photograph.** Sessions end immediately — that is real and tested — but the file and its row remain indefinitely, because nothing has been told what the retention period should be. And the only person entitled to remove it no longer has a login.

 **We deliberately did not choose.** Deleting on disable is our recommendation and it would have taken one line; building it would have answered a question that is yours. So it is written up for you instead, with the alternative (keep for a stated period, with a sweep) costed at the same place.

### What is NOT being asked of you here

 Nothing about client documents changed. The receipt strip, the quarantine discharge, the fail-closed settings and the six-year switch are all exactly as they were; this is a separate, smaller pipeline for a staff photograph and it reuses their store rather than their rules.

### For the developer — **timezone handling is built. Do not price it.**

 **What was actually wrong, so the size of the change is clear.** Every time in the booking system was stored, compared _and printed_ in UTC. Britain is UTC for about four months of the year and an hour ahead for the other eight, so a member of staff setting "09:00 to 17:00" in June was setting ten to six, and a client offered "Wednesday at 09:00" would have arrived at ten.  **What was built.** Storage and comparison stay in UTC — every stored moment is still an ISO-8601 `Z` string, and every value that crosses the wire still is. A member of staff's working hours are stored as **local** hours and converted per day when times are offered, because a person's hours are local hours and nobody should have to re-enter their week twice a year.

 **What is left, and it is a choice rather than work.** The display zone is a single constant, `Europe/London`, rather than a per-user setting — right for a business whose staff are in Britain and whose clients own British property.
