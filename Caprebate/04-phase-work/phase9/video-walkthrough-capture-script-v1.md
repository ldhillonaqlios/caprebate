## Part A — For the surveyor

### A.1 What we are asking you to do with this

Read Part B as if you were the person being asked to film, and tell us where it fails. Specifically:

1. **Is the order right?** We film exterior → plant → services intake → clinical rooms → circulation
   → ceiling and floor voids. We chose that order so the building-wide services are captured before
   the operator gets tired, and so the void shots come last because they are the fiddliest. If you
   would rather see clinical rooms first, say so.
2. **Have we asked them to open the wrong things?** We ask the operator to open the distribution
   board cover, lift one ceiling tile, and open the plant-room and comms-cabinet doors. Every one of
   those is a small risk — of damage, of an electrical hazard, of a client who cannot do it safely.
   **Tell us which of these you would drop, and which you would add.**
3. **Is §A.3 — what a video cannot establish — right and complete?** That section is the one we most
   want you to correct. **It is easier for you to amend a stated boundary than to invent one**, which
   is why we have written it as a position rather than as a question.
4. **Would you sign a valuation resting partly on this?** And if so, with what caveat, and above what
   size or complexity would you refuse?

### A.2 What the video is for, stated plainly

The camera captures what is **visible**. The hidden services — cabling in voids, sub-mains, drainage
and medical-gas runs behind walls — come from documents (M&E drawings, O&M manuals) and the cost
breakdown. **The video does not produce the figure and does not replace you.** It extends your reach,
lets us triage and scope a job, and hands you a structured evidence pack instead of a blank page.

### A.3 What a video CANNOT establish — our position, for you to correct

We would rather state this wrongly and be corrected than leave it blank.

| # | A video cannot… | Why | What we do instead |
|---|---|---|---|
| **V-1** | See **behind a panel or a wall** | The camera has no X-ray. Sub-mains, buried pipework, chair-position services under a dental floor, and anything in a wall cavity are invisible | M&E drawings, or a visit |
| **V-2** | See **above a suspended ceiling** beyond the one tile we ask them to lift | One tile shows what *kind* of services run in the void. It does not show extent, route or quantity | M&E drawings; the one-tile shot is a signpost, not a survey |
| **V-3** | See **below a raised or screeded floor** | Same reason. In a dental practice this is where the highest-value missed item lives (chair-position services) | Fit-out cost breakdown, or a visit |
| **V-4** | Establish anything requiring **touch** | Is that pipework insulated or lagged? Is that panel live? Is the benching fixed or freestanding? Is the flooring welded-seam or laid? **The fixture-vs-chattel call very often turns on whether something is physically fixed, and the camera cannot tell** | Ask the operator to narrate it, and treat the answer as a client assertion (grade C), not as observation |
| **V-5** | **Measure** anything reliably | A phone camera has no scale. Our scale-reference instruction (§B.2) gives an order of magnitude, not a dimension. A LiDAR scan, where the phone supports it, is materially better but still not a measured survey | Floor plans, EPC area, or your own measurement |
| **V-6** | Establish **age or condition** with any precision | A boiler looks like a boiler. Serial and data plates, which we do ask for, give age *where they are legible and where the plate is the original* | Data-plate shots, O&M manuals, and your judgement |
| **V-7** | Distinguish a **passenger lift from a through-floor / platform lift** on sight alone in every case | These sit in different pools (`SR` 6% vs `MP` 14%, taxonomy `LS-SR-005` vs `LS-CH-003`). The visual difference is real but not always obvious on phone footage | Explicit narration prompt, and the lift O&M / maintenance file |
| **V-8** | Tell us whether an item is **already on the accountant's fixed-asset register** | Nothing about the object says whether it has been claimed | The client's fixed-asset register — a document request, always |
| **V-9** | Resolve any **contested pool allocation** | Whether a UCV canopy, an IPS panel, a lead-lined imaging room or dental clinical flooring sits in `MP` or `SR` is a legal and professional judgement, not an observation. Perfect footage does not move it one inch | You, plus the CTA |
| **V-10** | Establish **what serves what** in a mixed building | In supported living, whether the plant serves the dwellings or the common parts decides whether the largest item on site is excluded in full. You can film a boiler; you cannot film where its pipework goes | M&E drawings, and the segregated cost breakdown |
| **V-11** | Cover anything the operator **did not point the camera at** | An absence in footage is not evidence of absence in the building. **This is the failure mode we are most worried about, because it looks like completeness** | The coverage score (§C) names what is missing rather than assuming it is not there |

**The honest summary we would put in front of a client:** a video tells you what is there and roughly
where. It does not tell you how much of it there is, how old it is, whether it is fixed, or which
pool it belongs in. **Three of those four are the things a claim actually turns on.**

### A.4 The data-protection constraint, which is not negotiable

Filming inside an operating clinic or care home risks capturing patients, residents and staff. The
script instructs **empty rooms, out of hours wherever possible**, and the capture flow must handle
consent and a lawful basis. **Caprebate becomes the controller for this footage.** This is
a design constraint, not a footnote, and it is why §B opens with it rather than closing with it.

## Part B — The script the client follows

> **This part is written to be handed to a practice manager or care-home manager. It assumes no
> technical knowledge. Read it out loud once before filming.**

### B.0 Before you start — please read this bit

**Film when the building is empty.** Out of hours, or a room at a time when nobody is in it. **Do not
film patients, residents, staff or anyone's records, screens or notes.** If someone walks into shot,
stop, wait, and start that room again. If you cannot empty a room, skip it and tell us — we would
much rather have a gap we know about than footage we have to delete.

**Do not put yourself at risk to get a shot.** Nothing on this list is worth a ladder you are not
comfortable on, a cover you are not qualified to remove, or a door you are not supposed to open.
**If a shot is not safe or not yours to take, skip it and say so on camera.** "I'm not opening this,
it's the landlord's" is a genuinely useful thing for us to hear.

**Three rules that matter more than the list:**

1. **Talk the whole time.** Say what room you are in as you walk in. Say what you are pointing at.
   Your voice is half the evidence — a silent video of a plant room is worth much less than a shaky
   one where you say "that's the boiler, that's the hot water cylinder, I don't know what that is".
   **"I don't know what that is" is a perfectly good thing to say.** Just point at it.
2. **One clip per room, and go slowly.** Walk the walls. Pause for two or three seconds on each
   thing. A single continuous clip beats twenty fragments.
3. **Lights on. Blinds open. Don't film into a window.**

**What you need:** your phone. That's it. A tape measure or a sheet of A4 helps (see B.2).
**Roughly how long:** 20–40 minutes for a dental practice or GP surgery; 60–90 for a care home or a
small hospital.

---

### B.1 The order — film it in this order, please

The order matters. It puts the building-wide things first, while you are fresh, and the fiddly bits
last.

```
1  OUTSIDE the building        →  2  PLANT ROOM
3  ELECTRICAL INTAKE           →  4  COMMS / DATA
5  WATER                       →  6  EVERY CLINICAL / CARE ROOM, in order
7  CIRCULATION & LIFE SAFETY   →  8  KITCHEN / LAUNDRY / UTILITY
9  CEILING & FLOOR VOIDS       →  10 ANYTHING YOU SKIPPED, said out loud
```

---

### B.2 Step 0 — the opening shot (30 seconds)

Stand outside the front door and say, to camera:

> "This is [practice / home name], [full address]. Today is [date]. I'm [your name], I'm the
> [your role]. This video was filmed with the building empty."

Then hold up an A4 sheet against the wall, or a tape measure extended to one metre, and film it for
three seconds. **This is our only scale reference — please do it.** If your phone has a
"Measure" app or a LiDAR scanner, use it on one room and tell us you have; that is much better than
the A4 sheet.

---

### B.3 Step 1 — Outside (2 minutes)

- **Walk the whole way round the building** if you can, filming continuously. Say which side you are
  on ("this is the back", "this is the car park side").
- Film the **whole building from far enough back to see it**, front and back.
- **Say what it's made of** if you know — "this is a 1970s brick building", "this bit's an
  extension, it went up about five years ago". **Guessing is fine, just say you're guessing.**
- Point the camera at any **external plant**: air-conditioning condenser units, a generator, oil or
  gas tanks, an oxygen or medical-gas store, a bin or plant compound. Count them out loud.
- Film the **roof edge** if any plant is visible up there. Do not go on the roof.

---

### B.4 Step 2 — The plant room / boiler room (4 minutes)

*This is the single most valuable room in the building for us. Spend time here.*

- Say **"this is the plant room"** as you go in.
- Film the **boiler(s)**. Get close to the **data plate** — the metal or sticker label with the model
  and serial number — and **hold the camera still on it for five seconds so it comes out readable.**
  If there is more than one boiler, do each.
- Film the **hot water cylinder / calorifier** and its data plate.
- Film the **pumps, valves, pressure vessels and the pipework runs** leaving the plant. Follow the
  pipes with the camera as far as you can see them and say where they go if you know.
- Film any **air-handling unit or ventilation plant**, and its data plate.
- Film the **controls panel** — the box with the screen or dials — and any **BMS** (building
  management system) panel.
- **Say how old you think it all is**, and whether any of it has been replaced. "The boiler went in
  about 2019, the cylinder's original" is exactly what we need.
- If there is a **standby generator or a UPS**, film it and its data plate.

---

### B.5 Step 3 — Electrical intake (3 minutes)

- Film the **meter(s)** — hold still on the reading and the serial number.
- Film the **main distribution board / consumer unit / fuse board**. **If the cover is a hinged door
  you can open without tools, open it and film the inside** — the row of breakers and the labels
  beside them. **If it needs a screwdriver, or if you are not comfortable, leave it shut and say so
  on camera.** Do not remove any panel that is screwed on.
- Film **any other distribution boards** around the building as you come to them, and say where they
  are.
- Film the **earthing point** and, if the building has one, any **lightning protection** connection
  you can see outside.

---

### B.6 Step 4 — Comms and data (2 minutes)

- Find the **comms cabinet or data hub** — usually a wall-mounted or floor-standing cabinet full of
  cables and small flashing boxes.
- **Open the door and film the inside.** Film the **patch panel** — the strip with lots of numbered
  sockets — and **hold still on it long enough for the numbers to be readable.**
- Say how many **data points** you think the building has, if you have any idea. If you don't, say
  so.
- Film the **fire alarm panel**, the **intruder alarm panel**, the **nurse-call or staff-call panel**,
  and the **CCTV recorder** — these are often in the same cupboard or near it. Film each panel's
  display and any zone list printed on or beside it. **A zone list is genuinely valuable to us.**

---

### B.7 Step 5 — Water (2 minutes)

- Film the **incoming water main and stopcock**.
- Film any **cold water storage tank** (usually in a roof space or a cupboard) — **only if you can
  reach it safely.**
- Film any **water treatment or softening plant**, and any **RO (reverse osmosis) unit** — common in
  dental decontamination rooms and hospital sterile services.
- Film any **thermostatic mixing valves** or a Legionella logbook if one is kept near the plant.

---

### B.8 Step 6 — Every clinical or care room, in order

*This is the longest part. Do the rooms in a sensible order — floor by floor, along the corridor —
and say the room's name and number as you walk in.*

**In every room, without exception, point at and name:**

- the **power sockets** (count them out loud)
- the **light fittings** (say whether they are recessed into the ceiling or surface-mounted)
- the **heating** — radiator, underfloor, ceiling unit
- any **ventilation grille or extract fan** in the ceiling or wall
- the **data / network sockets**
- the **switches, thermostats and any control panel**
- the **smoke or heat detector** on the ceiling (**say if there isn't one**)
- the **floor** — say what it is: "vinyl, welded at the edges", "carpet tiles", "safety flooring"
- the **fitted furniture** — worktops, cupboards, benching. **Say whether it is built in or
  freestanding. This one really matters to us and only you can tell us.**

**Then, the sector-specific things:**

**Do this first, before anything else — it decides everything.**

### B.9 Step 7 — Corridors, stairs and life safety (3 minutes)

Walk every corridor slowly with the camera running. As you go:

- **Point at each smoke or heat detector on the ceiling and count them out loud.** "One, two,
  three…" all the way along. **This is much more useful to us than you might think** — how many
  detection points a building has is one of the things we most often have to guess at.
- Film the **emergency light fittings** (the ones with a small green or red LED, or the green running-
  man boxes) and count those too.
- Film the **fire alarm call points** (the red break-glass boxes).
- Film any **sprinkler heads** in the ceiling.
- Film the **fire doors** — say whether they have **automatic closers or magnetic hold-opens**.
- Film the **CCTV cameras** and **access-control readers** on doors, and count them.
- Film the **signage** — fire exit signs and wayfinding.
- Film the **lift** doors on each floor, and the stairs.

---

### B.10 Step 8 — Kitchen, laundry and utility (2 minutes)

If you have not already covered these in your sector section:

- The **kitchen extract canopy** — from below, and say if it has a fan and where the duct goes.
- The **grease trap** if you know where it is.
- The **laundry equipment** and the **extract**.
- The **cleaner's cupboard / janitorial sink**.

---

### B.11 Step 9 — Ceiling and floor voids (3 minutes) — *last, and only if safe*

- **If the building has a suspended ceiling** (square tiles in a grid), **lift ONE tile in a corridor
  and ONE in a clinical room** and hold the camera up into the gap for ten seconds each. Pan slowly.
  **Do not stand on anything you are not confident on, and do not lift a tile you cannot put back.**
  If you can't do it, say so and skip it.
- **If the building has a raised access floor** (removable floor panels), say so — **do not lift
  one** unless you have the proper lifting tool and know it is safe.
- Say whether you know of any **service duct or riser cupboard**, and film the outside of the door.

---

### B.12 Step 10 — The last thing, and it is important

Film yourself, or just the corridor, and say out loud:

> **"Rooms I did not film, and why:"** — then list them. "I didn't do the two bedrooms on the first
> floor because residents were in them." "I didn't open the fuse board." "I couldn't get into the
> landlord's plant room."

**This is not a confession, it is data.** We would far rather know that a room was skipped than
assume the building doesn't have one. A gap we know about is a document request or a short visit.
**A gap we don't know about is a wrong answer.**

---

### B.13 The documents — the video is only half of it

**Please send these with the footage.** They contain everything the camera cannot see, and without
them the video is a much weaker piece of evidence:

| Document | Why we need it |
|---|---|
| **Floor plans** (any version, even a marked-up estate agent's plan) | Areas and room counts |
| **M&E drawings** (mechanical and electrical) | Everything behind the walls and above the ceilings |
| **O&M manuals** (operation and maintenance files, usually a lever-arch folder or a memory stick from the contractor) | Ages, models, and what was actually installed |
| **The fit-out or build cost breakdown** — the priced final account, not the headline figure | **The single most valuable document. Without it, everything is an estimate** |
| **Your fixed-asset register** (from your accountant) | So we do not claim something twice |
| **The lift maintenance file**, if you have a lift | Type, age, and whether it is a passenger or platform lift |
| **The fire risk assessment** | It usually lists rooms and often lists detection zones |
| **Any NHS or grant paperwork**, if the works were grant-funded | It decides what can be claimed at all |

## Part C — How the footage is scored

The list above is also the checklist. Every numbered item is tagged `required` or `optional`, and
the coverage score checks the uploaded footage against it: was each `required` area recorded and
named; is the lighting adequate; is it steady; is the audio intelligible; is there a scale reference;
is each clip long enough.

**What the score means, stated honestly:** it measures **capture-compliance against this script**,
not the accuracy of the eventual claim. **A 100% score means the surveyor has been handed good eyes.
It does not mean the figure is settled, and it must never be presented to a client as if it does.**

Missing a `required` item produces a specific re-shoot prompt ("the plant room and Surgery 3's chair
services weren't captured — please record these"), looping until coverage passes or the job is
escalated to a physical visit.

**Open — for the surveyor.** What should the pass mark be, how many re-shoot attempts before we stop
asking, and at what point does a persistent gap force a visit rather than another retry? We have not
set these numbers because they are a judgement about evidential sufficiency, which is yours.
*(Question Q-VW-3 below.)*

## Part D — Questions this puts to the surveyor

Additions to the plain-English question set on item 12933605005.

**Q-VW-1 — Is §A.3 right?** We have written eleven things a video cannot establish. **Which of them
is wrong, and what have we missed?** We would rather you struck one out than added one, because a
boundary we have overstated costs us jobs and a boundary we have understated costs us a claim.

**Q-VW-2 — What have we asked the client to open that they should not?** We ask them to open a
distribution board cover, a comms cabinet, and to lift a ceiling tile. Each is a small risk. **Which
would you drop?**

**Q-VW-3 — What is a passing video?** What coverage threshold, how many re-shoots, and what forces a
visit? We have deliberately not set numbers.

**Q-VW-4 — Would you sign a valuation resting partly on this footage, and with what caveat?** And is
there a building size, sector or complexity above which the answer is simply no?

**Q-VW-5 — Is the order in §B.1 right?** Exterior → plant → intake → rooms → circulation → voids.
Would you rather the clinical rooms came first?

**Q-VW-6 — The counting asks.** We ask the operator to count detection points, emergency lights,
CCTV cameras and access readers out loud as they walk. That is unusual and it lengthens the video.
**Is a client's own count worth having, or would you disregard it anyway?** If you would disregard
it, we will take it out — it is the single most tedious instruction in the script.
