# What we think is in each room

## The plain room list, sector by sector — for a chartered surveyor to mark up

**Caprebate · version 1.0 · 2 September 2026**
**This answers Question 3 of the surveyor question pack in advance. You will have it in front of you when we ask.**

---

## How to use this document

Every room type we model is listed below, sector by sector, with the equipment we assume is in it. **There is a blank column on the right of every table. Please write in it.** Cross something out, add something we have missed, or put a question mark against anything that looks wrong. A tidy answer is worth less to us than a marked-up one.

**You do not need to read anything else first.** There is a longer technical version behind this, with classification codes and pool allocations, but nothing here depends on it. If a mark-up of yours turns out to need the technical version, we will bring it to you.

**What is deliberately not here: numbers.** Nothing below says "three sluice rooms" or "twelve nurse-call points". A count is a fact about one specific building and we have not seen one. The **What drives the count** column says what a count *would* be measured against. The count itself is the survey's job.

---

## The two columns that matter most

### "How sure we are"

We have graded every row. **The grade is the point of the document** — it tells you where to spend your time.

| Grade | What it means | What we would like from you |
|---|---|---|
| **Sourced** | It is written down in something we hold: one of our own sector specifications, or a named technical standard. | Confirm it is not out of date. Low priority. |
| **Standard practice** | Not written down anywhere we hold, but we believe it is how these buildings are ordinarily built. | Tell us if it is wrong. Medium priority. |
| **Reasoned** | Worked out from what the room is *for*. Not from a source and not from observed practice. Plausible; unverified. | **Please check these.** |
| **May not exist** | We are not confident the room, or the item, occurs in this sector at all — or at typical scale. | Tell us: always, sometimes, or never. |
| **⚠ GUESS** | **We had nothing better and we are saying so rather than dressing it up.** These are kept out of the lists, in their own marked blocks. | **These first.** |

### "What drives the count"

Four possibilities, and which one applies is a real decision, not a formality.

| Driver | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Per room** | Counted room by room. Includes per bed, per chair and per treatment position — anything driven by what a space needs rather than by how big the building is. |
| **Per building** | One installation for the whole site, however big it is. |
| **Per square metre** | Spread across the whole floor area, not counted room by room. |
| **From the paperwork** | Not derived at all. Read off a priced bill of quantities or the client's fixed-asset register. |

**⟳ marks the rows where we changed our method.** We used to derive these from floor area. We now count them room by room, on our director's instruction to over-engineer rather than under-engineer.

The change matters more than it sounds. Take a 268 m² dental practice: the floor-area rule gave **two to five** smoke detectors. Counting the rooms gives **fifteen to nineteen**, because a 14 m² surgery needs its own detector whether the building is 268 m² or 2,680 m². The area rule was not slightly wrong — it was answering a different question. We have applied the same reasoning to data outlets and light fittings, which have the same shape.

**This is decided and built. What is still yours** is the more useful half: for each thing we count, is it *genuinely* driven by room count, by floor area, or by something else — and **where is a rule of thumb good enough?** "Over-engineered" is our director's instruction, not a claim that more precision is always better, and you are better placed than either of us to say where it turns into false precision.

# What this document does not say, and why

- **No counts.** Nothing here says how many of anything is in any building. A count is a fact about one property; we have not seen one.
- **No costs or rates.** Different document.
- **No resolution of the contested classifications.** Where a row is marked contested, we have carried the flag through rather than settling it. Settling it is a surveyor-and-tax-adviser call, and settling it wrongly is a permanent error rather than a rounding one.
- **Our room-by-room shape is our drafting choice, not a source.** We modelled it on a fire risk assessment, because that is the room-level paperwork a client is most likely to already hold. **A fire risk assessment is organised around fire risk, not around equipment value, and its groupings may not be the most useful ones here.** If you would rather this were organised around the M&E schedule or the client's own room schedule, **say so — restructuring is cheap now and expensive later.**
