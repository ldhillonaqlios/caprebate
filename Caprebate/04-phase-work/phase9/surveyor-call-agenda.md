# Straw-man — Surveyor Call Agenda: Free 45-Minute Intro Call (v0.1)

**Monday item 12830181616** — "Book a free 45-minute call with a surveyor before we build anything
else."

> **THIS IS A DRAFT AGENDA FOR A CALL WITH A CHARTERED SURVEYOR WHO HAS NOT YET BEEN APPOINTED.**
> It was drafted by a non-surveyor (Lakhbir) preparing to run the call, without professional input.
> Nothing in it is professional advice, and no answer given informally on a free scoping call should
> be treated as a signed professional position — it is intelligence-gathering to shape the *build*,
> not a sign-off. Where the surveyor gives a number or a rule of thumb, write it down as **provisional
> — pending formal engagement**, not as settled policy.

**Purpose of this specific call:** this call exists to close the `[SURVEYOR'S CALL]` blanks left in
`phase9/strawman-desktop-first-policy.md` — the desktop-first policy governing when a site visit can
be replaced by a video walkthrough. Every question below says which blank it is trying to close. If
the surveyor pushes back on the *shape* of that policy (the axes, the tiers, the grid), that is a
good outcome, not a distraction — capture it and revise the policy document afterwards.

## 1. The few decisions we most need out of this call

Named up front, because 45 minutes is not enough time to discuss everything below and it is easy to
run out the clock on preamble. If nothing else lands, these should:

1. **Whether "video instead of a visit" is something this surveyor would sign against at all**, and
   for which sectors/job types — a flat "no, never" materially changes the whole build, not just this
   policy document.
2. **A first cut at the job-value bands** that separate desktop-only from video-required from
   visit-required (policy §2.2).
3. **Which of the nine `D`/`E`-graded fields** (GIA, construction type, residential split, pool tag,
   present/quantity/cost-basis per fixture, fixture-vs-chattel) they would actually accept on video
   evidence, and which they would not (policy §4.1).
4. **Whether they will take this on at all**, and on what basis (retainer, per-job fee, pilot
   arrangement) — this is a business decision riding alongside the technical one, and the call is
   also, frankly, an interview.

## 1A. ⬛ ADDED 1 SEPTEMBER 2026 (Phase 63) — three items that must stay on this agenda, and one that is now a pair

**Lakhbir answered five of the surveyor-pack questions himself on 1 September. Two of those answers
do NOT close their questions, and the risk is that a closed-looking board entry quietly removes them
from this call. They stay.**

### 1A.1 — I1 and I2 stay on the agenda. The answer given was a commercial preference, not the answer asked for

Both were answered **"yes — a desktop survey"**. That says what we would like to sell. **The
questions ask whether a desk estimate is *defensible*, and that is a professional judgement about
the surveyor’s own liability, not a commercial choice about our pricing.** Only they can answer it.

**Ask it as put:** where does a desk estimate stop being defensible — is there a size, a complexity,
or a category of plant above which somebody has to attend?

### 1A.2 — The consequence to put in front of them, because it removes our fallback

**There is no minimum job size** (decided separately, 1 September). A site visit on a small
percentage fee is a loss. **So no-minimum plus desktop-only commits us to desk-only working on small
jobs, with no visit to fall back on.**

That is coherent as a business model, and it puts the entire weight on the desk method. **If the
honest professional answer is "not defensible below a certain quality of input", we would rather
change the product than the answer** — but we need to hear it on this call, not after a complaint.

### 1A.3 — Put I4 and I5 as a PAIR, not as two answers

**This is the one most likely to be got wrong by presenting the answers in the order they were
given.**

- **I4 (counting):** answered *"I would rather this be over engineered"* → count by room, not by
  floor area. **More precision.** Decided and built; the quantity model is at v3.
- **I5 (ageing):** answered *"one reduction across the whole building"* → a single building-wide
  factor rather than per-element lives. **Less precision.**

**They pull against each other.** The combination may be exactly right — counting is cheap once a
room list exists, whereas per-element ageing needs plant lives we do not hold and would be false
precision stacked on estimated quantities. **But it is a combination, and asking about either half
alone invites an answer to the wrong question.** Ask: *is it defensible to be deliberately precise
in quantity and deliberately coarse in ageing, in the same valuation?*

## 4. Questions, in priority order

Each question states **why we're asking**, **what a good answer looks like** (so the non-surveyor
running the call can tell a real answer from a polite deflection), and **which policy blank it
closes**.

### 4.1 The core question (§2.2 of the timebox)

**Q1. In your own practice, have you ever signed a capital allowances valuation without physically
attending the property — and if so, under what circumstances?**

- *Why we're asking:* this is the single fact that determines whether the desktop-first policy is
  worth building at all, versus being told plainly that it isn't something a RICS surveyor will
  stand behind.
- *What a good answer looks like:* a specific example or a specific rule ("only for a straightforward
  fit-out under about £X, with full invoices, where I know the practice already"), not a flat "it
  depends" with no content behind it. A firm "never" is also a good, usable answer — it tells us the
  policy's ceiling is DT-T3, not DT-T1/DT-T2, for this surveyor.
- *Closes:* the premise of the whole policy document (§1, §2.4 grid ceiling).

**Q2. If a client sends you a well-shot video walkthrough — narrated, well-lit, following a
structured shot list — what would that video need to show you before you'd trust it over your own
eyes?**

- *Why we're asking:* this reframes the question away from "would you skip a visit" (which invites
  a reflexive no) toward "what would convince you," which surfaces the actual evidentiary bar.
- *What a good answer looks like:* specific, nameable things — "I'd want to see the plant room lit
  properly and a scale reference in every room" — not a general "it would need to be very good."
- *Closes:* policy §3 (evidence required per tier) and §5 (escalation triggers).

### 4.2 The grid (§2.3 of the timebox — the main event)

**Q3. Does splitting jobs by "value" and "complexity" as two separate axes match how you actually
think about risk on a job, or would you structure it differently?**

- *Why we're asking:* the straw-man's whole grid depends on this being the right shape. If the
  surveyor's mental model is different (e.g. "I only care about the trigger type — purchase vs
  fit-out — value barely matters"), that should reshape the document, not just fill in its blanks.
- *What a good answer looks like:* either "yes, that's roughly right" with adjustments, or a
  clearly-stated alternative structure we can act on.
- *Closes:* policy §2.1, §2.4.

**Q4. Roughly where would you draw the job-value bands — the size of job below which you'd never
insist on a visit, and above which you always would, regardless of anything else?**

- *Why we're asking:* this is the most concrete blank in the whole document.
- *What a good answer looks like:* actual numbers, even approximate ones ("under £30–50k I wouldn't
  normally need to go, over £250k I always would"), rather than "it varies too much to say."
- *Closes:* policy §2.2 (`V1`–`V4` bands).

**Q5. Which of these factors would actually change your mind about needing to attend, and which
wouldn't: the type of job (purchase vs fit-out vs new build), the sector, the age of the building,
whether an O&M manual or fire plan exists, and whether there's a live s.198 election?**

- *Why we're asking:* the straw-man proposes seven complexity factors (policy §2.3) without any
  confidence they're the right ones or correctly weighted.
- *What a good answer looks like:* the surveyor ranking or discarding factors — "trigger type matters
  a lot, building age barely at all, the O&M manual matters more than people think" — giving us a
  real ranking, not agreement with all seven by default.
- *Closes:* policy §2.3 (factor list and weighting).

**Q6. Here is a test we would like you to try to break. A desktop pack is enough to sign on when every fact we always show you is filled in from a named document or public register, with where it came from and how far it can be trusted printed beside it — and your remaining work is only the figures we hold back until you have committed your own, plus the declaration. A site visit is needed when one of those facts cannot be filled in from any document. Does that test hold in your practice, and where does it fail?**

- *What "the facts we always show you" means:* the client's answers and uploaded documents; public-register data (Land Registry, EPC, Valuation Office, CQC, Companies House); the gross internal area and floor plans; the room schedule and fixture inventory; title, tenure, price paid and completion date. **What we hold back until you have committed your own figures:** the qualifying expenditure, the qualifying percentage, the main-pool / special-rate split, the bare-site land value, the apportionment multiplier and the indicative lifetime saving.
- *Why we're asking:* **Sharpened 14 September 2026 (Phase 131).** The earlier question 6 asked which of the nine hard-to-verify fields they would accept from a video, which produces a tier without naming what is missing. This test can be checked before a job starts, names the specific fact that failed, and gives the same answer whoever applies it. It is proposed as a replacement for the value-and-complexity grid in the desktop-first policy, not an addition to it.
- *Where we expect it to fail, so listen for it:* the facts most often missing from documents are the construction type, the true gross internal area, and whether plant that is built in and undocumented is actually there. On a purchase, two inputs — an elemental cost library and a bare-site land value from comparables — are things we do not hold at all.
- *What a good answer looks like:* the test accepted with named exceptions ("fine for a fit-out with a final account; never on a purchase"), or the specific fact that breaks it. **If they will not engage with a test at all, fall back to the earlier question:** of the internal floor area, the construction type, the split between fixed and mobile equipment, and which pool a fixture falls into, which would they accept from a video and which would they always want to measure or see themselves? A blanket "I'd need to see everything" is a valid but very different answer — write it down as such.
- *Closes:* policy §4.1 (the nine-field list) directly, and tests the proposal to replace the grid with a document test.

### 4.3 Escalation and the video threshold (§2.4 of the timebox)

**Q7. Are there situations where you'd insist on a visit no matter how small or simple the job looks
on paper — a connected-party sale, a sale-and-leaseback, a disputed election, a contested "is this a
dwelling" question?**

- *Why we're asking:* testing the escalation-trigger list (policy §5) for completeness and for
  anything the surveyor would actually treat as gridable rather than an automatic override.
- *What a good answer looks like:* confirmation of the list as drafted, additions, or an explicit
  "actually I'd be relaxed about X" that lets us shrink the trigger list.
- *Closes:* policy §5.

**Q8. If a client's video is reviewed and scored for completeness, what pass mark would you want to
see before you'd rely on it — and how many times would you let them re-film before you said "I need
to come and see this myself"?**

- *Why we're asking:* this is a named open item in the existing Video Capture Protocol (§7) as well
  as this policy — nobody has set a number yet.
- *What a good answer looks like:* any concrete number or range, even a rough one — "90%-plus, and
  I wouldn't want more than two re-shoot rounds before it's clearly not going to work."
- *Closes:* policy §5 (coverage-score trigger); Video Capture Protocol §7.

### 4.4 Other open surveyor-owned questions — raise if time allows (§2.5 of the timebox)

**Q9. Valuation basis: which basis should the valuation use — replacement cost for the plant and the building and open market value for the bare site, or something else — and if replacement cost, new or depreciated? Does the answer differ for a bought building against a self-built or fitted-out one?**

- *Why we're asking:* **Corrected 14 September 2026 (Phase 131).** This used to point at the survey-pack wireframe's "Depreciation applied" step, and the note recording this agenda on the board said the valuation basis was "defaulted to depreciated". Both read as though our figures are depreciated. They are not. **Both depreciation methods — per element and whole building — are built and ship switched off, so no depreciation is applied today.** The valuation-basis switch carries a depreciated-replacement-cost label by default, but with depreciation off it produces exactly the replacement-new figures, and the output says no depreciation was applied. **Which basis to use is an open question for the surveyor, and we have deliberately not chosen it.** For comparison, a competitor's report we hold uses replacement cost for the plant and the building, with no age deduction, and open market value for the bare site.
- *What a good answer looks like:* a stated basis for each part (plant and building; bare site), a default on depreciation with the exceptions named ("I'd normally use depreciated replacement cost for anything more than a few years old"), and whether a purchase differs from a self-built or fitted-out building.
- *Closes:* the open question "Which valuation basis should we be using?" — not part of the desktop-first grid; record the answer there. Source for the earlier wording: `caprebate-desktop-survey-pack-wireframe-v6.html` §9.

**Q10. Room-schedule and quantity-derivation coefficients: our fixture taxonomy derives quantities
of some items from room counts (e.g. a per-bedroom, per-surgery, per-bed, or per-theatre count) rather
than a direct measurement. Are there sector rules of thumb you use for how many of an item (sockets,
call points, basins, gas outlets) to expect per room or per bed, that we could use as defaults —
flagged as estimates, subject to your correction on each job?**

- *Why we're asking:* the taxonomy (`Caprebate-Fixture-Taxonomy-v1.csv`, `quantity_basis` column)
  already keys several rows to `room schedule`, `surgery (chair) count`, `bed count` and
  `theatre count` rather than to a measured quantity, but no coefficient values exist yet.
- *What a good answer looks like:* even one or two concrete examples ("two nurse-call points per
  bedroom is typical in a care home") gives us a starting default to flag as provisional.
- *Closes:* a separate open item feeding the fixture taxonomy, not the desktop-first grid — record
  as its own action. Source: `Caprebate-Fixture-Taxonomy-v1.csv`; `Build-Prep-Consolidated-Schema.md`
  §1.C `room_counts` (grade C).

### 4.5 Commercials and next steps (§2.6 of the timebox)

**Q11. Would you be willing to review and mark up the desktop-first policy document itself after this
call, and on what basis (retainer, day rate, per-job fee, or a short paid scoping piece)?**

- *Why we're asking:* the call is a scoping conversation, not an engagement — we need to know the
  route from "had a useful chat" to "have an appointed surveyor correcting our documents."
- *What a good answer looks like:* a concrete next step with a rough basis attached, even
  provisional.
- *Closes:* not a policy blank — this closes the "surveyor not yet appointed" gap the whole exercise
  exists because of.
