Can private hospitals and clinics claim capital allowances?

The short answer

Usually, yes. Capital allowances for private hospitals and clinics can be claimed by a business that pays UK tax and paid for qualifying fixtures, such as theatre ventilation, medical gases, lifts and imaging shielding. A registered charity cannot claim. Who claims, and how much, depends on who owns the building and how the spend happened.

Most of the value sits in the services, not the structure. A chartered surveyor values each item and signs the split of costs, and we work alongside your accountant.

Key points

  • The business must pay UK income tax or corporation tax. A charity cannot claim.
  • Most qualifying items are services: ventilation, medical gases, power, lifts, shielding and sterile services.
  • The frame, floors and walls are not plant, and land never qualifies.
  • An operator that leases its building can usually claim on the fit-out it paid for.
  • Fixtures can only be claimed once, so we check what has been claimed already.
  • The assessment is free. Our fee is a flat 3% of the allowances we identify.

How you came to own your hospital changes the claim

The same theatre can be claimed in four different ways. What matters depends on how the spend happened.

View B1 · Bought(01)

You bought a hospital or clinic

Part of the price can be for qualifying fixtures. What matters is whether a past owner claimed and any value fixed with the seller. A seller that could not claim, such as a property investor or an NHS body, can open a fuller claim. Buying the company's shares is not itself a claim.

View B2 · Built(02)

You built or extended

A new build is claimed from the cost breakdown and the mechanical and electrical packages, with a share of fees. A company may get relief on new plant in the first year. The structure itself may get the structures and buildings allowance.

You refurbished a theatre or ward

Stripping out and upgrading a theatre usually counts as capital, so it can qualify. Like-for-like repairs may be deducted in full instead. Repairs to a building bought run-down, before clinical use, usually count as capital.

View B4 · Fitted out(04)

You fitted out a lease

Many hospitals are sold to an investor and leased back. The operator that pays for the fit-out can usually claim, even on a short lease. If the landlord paid part of it, that part comes off.

A guide to capital allowances for private hospitals and clinics

For owners, operators, finance directors and consultant partners who want to know what can be claimed, who can claim it and how.

01 · The basicsWhat are capital allowances for private hospitals and clinics?

Capital allowances are a tax relief on money a business spends on plant and machinery. In a building, that includes many fixtures: the systems that make the building work for the trade carried on inside it. The allowances reduce taxable profits, so they lower income tax or corporation tax. They do not reduce capital gains tax.

A private hospital is one of the most fixture-heavy buildings there is. Theatres, imaging rooms, sterile services and critical care all depend on fixed plant. That is why capital allowances for private hospitals are worth a close look, even when the accounts already show a large equipment claim.

This guide covers the full range of private healthcare premises, including:

  • private acute hospitals and day-surgery units
  • diagnostic and imaging centres
  • fertility, dialysis, oncology, eye and endoscopy clinics
  • cosmetic and aesthetics clinics
  • consulting-room and outpatient clinics

A small consulting-room clinic behaves much like a dental practice fit-out or a GP surgery claim.

02 · Who can claimWho can claim private hospital tax relief?

Two things must be true. The business must pay UK income tax or corporation tax on its profits. And it must have paid for the qualifying items itself, as owner or as tenant.

Charities and not-for-profit operators

Some well-known hospital operators are registered charities. A charity does not pay the tax that capital allowances reduce, so it cannot claim, however large the building spend. Many hospices are in the same position. A trading subsidiary may have its own tax position, so we check that too.

Companies, partnerships and sole traders

Most private hospitals are run by companies. Consultant-owned clinics are sometimes partnerships or LLPs. What qualifies is the same for each. What changes is which reliefs apply and how fast they arrive, because some of the fastest are for companies only. Section 08 explains the difference.

A small business that keeps its accounts on the cash basis cannot claim on property fixtures.

03 · What qualifiesWhat qualifies in a private hospital or clinic fit-out?

We start with the systems in the qualifying list above.

  • Building services. The electrical installation and lighting, cold water, heating, general ventilation and cooling, and passenger lifts. These are known as integral features. They qualify, but the relief comes through more slowly.
  • General fixtures. Washrooms and sanitaryware, fitted furniture, fire alarms, security and access control, data cabling, signage, blinds and specialist clinical flooring.
  • Clinical plant. Medical gas pipework and outlets, theatre pendants and scrub-up stations, fixed operating lights, sterile services and decontamination plant, aseptic pharmacy and laboratory fit-out, nurse call, patient hoists and specialist lifts, pneumatic tube systems, hydrotherapy pool plant, kitchens, laundries and mortuary refrigeration.

Building work done so that plant can be installed can count too. Forming a plant room or riser for medical gases, or strengthening a ceiling to carry a theatre canopy, can be treated as part of the plant it serves. Work that only houses plant does not count in the same way.

Worth knowing

Appearing on a list in the tax rules does not make an item qualify by itself. For many fixtures, the test is what the item does in your trade. That is why we look at each item in your building, not a standard list.

04 · Theatres and imagingCapital allowances for theatre ventilation and MRI suites

Operating theatre ventilation

Ultra-clean ventilation over the operating table, and pressure-controlled ventilation in isolation and critical care rooms, can qualify. The question is usually not whether it qualifies, but how. Treated as clinical apparatus, the relief can come through faster. Treated as part of the building's general ventilation, the relief is slower. HMRC may take the slower view.

The same question comes up for isolated power supplies in theatres and critical care, and for imaging shielding. A chartered surveyor and a tax specialist make each call and record the evidence behind it.

MRI suites, CT and X-ray rooms

The scanner itself is normally loose equipment, and your accountant may already have claimed it. The room around it is different. Lead-lined walls, doors and glass for X-ray and CT, the shielded room around an MRI scanner, and the dedicated services that feed them are fixtures. These are often missed.

Building alterations needed to install the scanner can also count with it. We count each item once, and never the same cost twice.

05 · BuyingBuying a private hospital or clinic: what can you claim?

When you buy a building, part of the price can be for qualifying fixtures. How much you can claim depends on the building's history.

  • Did a past owner claim? If so, special rules decide what passes to you, and there may be a cap.
  • Was a value fixed with the seller? A fixtures election records the value of fixtures passing to you. A low figure can cap your claim.
  • Could the seller claim at all? A property investor, a pension fund, an NHS body or a developer often could not. That can open a fresh, fuller claim, although an earlier owner who could claim may still limit it.
  • When did the seller buy? Before April 2008, some building services could not be claimed. That can mean an extra slice is available to you now.
  • Did you buy shares? Buying the company that owns the hospital is not itself a claim. Later building work still counts.

The buyer has to show the claim is right. The sale contract and the pre-contract enquiry replies often record the position, so keep them. Transfers between connected businesses can limit what is claimable, so we have a specialist check the chain.

Fixtures in a building you bought are second-hand. Full expensing does not apply to them, but other allowances do. Compare the four routes in how you came to own it.

06 · Building and refurbishingBuilding, extending or refurbishing a private hospital

New builds and extensions

A new build or extension is claimed from the cost breakdown and the mechanical and electrical packages, with a fair share of preliminaries and professional fees. The structure itself is not plant. It may get the structures and buildings allowance instead, a slower, flat-rate relief for non-residential buildings.

Refurbishments: repairs or improvements?

Stripping out and upgrading a theatre or ward usually counts as capital, so it can qualify for capital allowances. Replacing parts like for like is more likely a repair. A genuine repair can often be deducted in full straight away, which is faster still. Two points matter:

  • Repairs to a building bought run-down, done before clinical use, usually count as capital.
  • Large-scale work on one building system, such as the ventilation, electrics, heating or lifts, can be treated as capital once it passes about half the cost of replacing that system within twelve months.

Contaminated or derelict sites

A company that removes asbestos or cleans up contamination it did not cause may get a separate, extra deduction.

07 · Leases and leasebacksCan a tenant claim on a clinic fit-out, or after a sale and leaseback?

Many hospitals are sold to an investor and leased back, and many clinics, including aesthetics clinics, trade from leased units. In both cases the tenant can often claim.

Who owns the buildingWho can usually claim
The operatorThe operator, on the building's fixtures and its own fit-out.
A property investor, with the operator as tenantThe operator, on the fit-out and plant it paid for.
A pension scheme or an NHS bodyThe tenant, on the fit-out it paid for. The owner cannot use the allowances.
Any owner, with a registered charity running the hospitalUsually not the charity. We check for a trading subsidiary.

A tenant can claim on the fit-out it pays for, even on a fairly short lease. A formal lease helps. A licence or informal arrangement needs a specialist look first. If the landlord paid part of the fit-out, that part comes off the tenant's claim.

Sale and leaseback

Selling a hospital and leasing it back counts as a sale for tax. It brings in a fixtures election and can mean a balancing charge for the seller. It is best reviewed at heads of terms, before the deal completes, and we treat it as its own piece of work.

Clinics in converted houses

The rules that limit claims on dwellings do not apply to a dedicated clinical building. If a clinic is in a converted house or has a flat above it, we take the residential part out of the claim.

08 · Reliefs and timingWhich reliefs apply, and how far back can a clinic claim?

Once an item qualifies, the next question is which relief applies. On a large project, the order matters.

  • Full expensing. Companies can deduct the full cost of new main-rate plant in the first year.
  • First-year allowance on special-rate items. Companies can get part of the cost of new building services relieved in the first year.
  • Annual investment allowance. Open to all businesses, up to a yearly limit that a group shares. It is often best aimed at the slower items.
  • Writing-down allowances. These cover the balance, and all second-hand fixtures, over a number of years.

Partnerships and sole traders use the annual investment allowance, writing-down allowances and the first-year allowance open to them.

How far back you can go

It depends on dates. If the tax return for the year of the spend can still be amended, relief can come sooner, sometimes as a refund. If it cannot, fixtures in a building you still own can usually still be claimed, with the relief spread over later years.

VAT, grants and donations

Private clinical care is largely VAT-exempt, so VAT you cannot reclaim can count as part of the cost. If you also do cosmetic or NHS-contract work, the share you can reclaim needs checking first. Spend paid for by a grant or donation cannot also be claimed.

Questions about capital allowances for private hospitals

Note 01Can a charity-run hospital claim capital allowances?

Usually not. A registered charity does not pay the tax that capital allowances reduce. A trading subsidiary that runs part of the operation may have its own tax position and its own claim.

Note 02We sold our hospital and leased it back. Who can claim?

The operator that leases the building back can usually claim on the fit-out and plant it pays for, because the investor's rental income is often tax-free. The sale itself counts as a disposal for tax, so the fixtures election and any balancing charge need care before the deal completes.

Note 03Does operating theatre ventilation qualify?

It can. Ultra-clean theatre ventilation, and pressure ventilation in isolation and critical care rooms, can qualify. The harder question is how fast the relief comes, and HMRC may take a different view. A chartered surveyor and a tax specialist make that call, with the evidence.

Note 04Our accountant already claimed the scanners. Is anything left?

Often, yes. A scanner or a surgical robot is loose equipment. The room around it, its shielding and its fixed services are fixtures, and these are often missed. Fixtures can only be claimed once, so we check your fixed-asset register first.

Note 05Can a small consulting-room clinic claim?

Yes, if the business pays tax and paid for qualifying fixtures. A consulting-room fit-out is closer to a dental or GP practice, so the claim is usually smaller. The free assessment tells you early if it is worth going on.

Note 06We are a partnership of consultants. Does that change anything?

Yes. What qualifies is the same, but some of the fastest reliefs, such as full expensing, are for companies only, so the timing can differ. We work alongside your accountant on which applies.

Note 07Can a tenant claim capital allowances on a clinic fit-out?

Usually, yes. A tenant that pays UK tax can claim on the fit-out and plant it paid for, even on a fairly short lease. If the landlord paid part of it, that part comes off. A licence or informal arrangement needs a specialist look first.

Note 08Does an MRI suite qualify for capital allowances?

The room can. The scanner is usually loose equipment, claimed separately. The shielded room around it, lead lining in X-ray and CT rooms, and their dedicated services are fixtures that can qualify. How fast the relief comes is a call for a chartered surveyor and a tax specialist.

Note 09Can an aesthetics or cosmetic clinic claim capital allowances?

Yes, if the business pays UK tax and paid for qualifying fixtures, such as the electrics, lighting, heating, ventilation, washrooms and fitted furniture. Cosmetic work is often standard-rated for VAT, which affects whether VAT on the spend can be counted, so we check that first.

Note 10How far back can a private hospital claim capital allowances?

It depends on dates. If the tax return for the year of the spend can still be amended, relief can come sooner, sometimes as a refund. If not, fixtures in a building you still own can usually still be claimed, with the relief spread over later years.
