In brief

Can a dental practice claim capital allowances?

The short answer

Usually, yes. Capital allowances for dental practices cover the suction and air plant, the services piped and wired to each chair, the decontamination room and the building's own systems. They reduce the practice's taxable profit. The built-in services are often never claimed, even when the loose dental equipment has been.

What you can claim depends on how you came to the practice: a squat fit-out, a refurbishment, a property purchase or a share purchase. If there is no claim, we tell you early, in writing.

Key points

  • Fitting out a new (squat) practice or refurbishing one is the most common dental claim.
  • Your accountant has usually claimed the chairs and loose equipment. The pipes, power and plant behind them are often missed.
  • Buying the shares in a practice company is not itself a claim. Buying the property can be.
  • A SIPP or SSAS pension cannot claim. The practice claims its own fit-out.
  • A dental practice is a trade, so only a flat above, or any part used as a home, is taken out.
  • A chartered surveyor signs every split of costs. Free assessment, flat 3% fee.

How you came to own it

How you came to the dental practice changes the claim

Dental practices change hands often, and many are owned through a pension.

Bought

You bought a practice

Buying the shares in a practice company is not itself a claim, but any fixtures claim the company never made may still be there. If you bought the property, part of the price can be for fixtures. What the seller claimed, and any fixed value agreed for the fixtures in the sale, both matter.

Built

You built or extended

A new building holds the same surgery systems, but the structure makes up more of the cost, so a smaller share qualifies. The structure may get a separate, smaller allowance.

Refurbished

You refurbished

Stripping out and re-fitting a surgery is usually capital spending. Like-for-like renewal can be a repair, deducted in full straight away. Repairs to run-down premises done before you see patients are usually capital.

Fitted out

You fitted out a lease, or your pension owns it

You can claim the fit-out you paid for, even on a short lease. If your SIPP or SSAS owns the building, the practice claims its own fit-out. A lease from your own pension should be at a market rent. A landlord's contribution reduces the claim.

01 · The basicsWhat are capital allowances for dental practices?

Capital allowances are a tax relief on money a business spends on plant and machinery. In a dental practice, that means more than the chairs and the X-ray machine. Much of the plant is built into the building: the pipes, wiring, pumps and fittings that make each surgery work.

The allowances reduce the practice's taxable profit. A sole trader or partner saves income tax. A practice company saves corporation tax. How much tax you save depends on your own tax rate, so our figures show the allowances, not the tax.

Who can claim

Any dental practice that pays tax can usually claim: a sole trader, a partnership, an LLP or a company. Some faster reliefs are for companies only, and many dentists now practise through a company. A practice that keeps its accounts on the cash basis cannot claim on property fixtures.

A dental practice is a trade, so the rule that limits claims on homes does not usually apply. The one exception is a flat above the surgery, or any part of the building used as a home. That part is taken out of the claim.

02 · What qualifiesWhat qualifies in a dental surgery fit-out?

The value is mostly in the services behind the clinical work. In a dental practice these usually include:

  • the central suction pump and the aspiration pipe ring to each surgery
  • the oil-free compressor and the air lines to the handpieces
  • dedicated power, water, drainage, air and suction at each chair
  • fixed operating lights
  • the decontamination room: fixed washer-disinfectors and sterilisers, purified water, sinks, benches and drains
  • X-ray shielding in surgeries and in OPG or CBCT scanner rooms
  • amalgam separators, where one is still fitted, and sedation gas pipes, where sedation is offered
  • the building's own systems: heating, hot and cold water, ventilation, air conditioning, lighting and wiring
  • fire alarms, CCTV, door entry and surgery call systems

The building work needed to put these systems in can count too, such as chasing walls for the pipe runs or forming the plant room. Each piece of that work is tied to the system it serves.

03 · Equipment or fixturesCapital allowances on dental equipment, and the services often missed

Most dental accountants already claim the loose equipment: chairs, X-ray and CBCT machines, intraoral scanners, freestanding autoclaves, instruments and computers. That is right, and we do not claim it again.

The built-in services behind that equipment are a different matter. The suction ring main, the air lines and the power, water and drainage at each chair are part of the building. They are often never claimed, because they sit inside a fit-out invoice as one large figure.

We start with your accountant's fixed asset register and past capital allowances workings. Then we add only what is missing, so each item is claimed once.

Worth knowing

The dental chair is the classic borderline item. It is plant either way, but whether it counts as a fixture or as loose equipment is a judgement. That matters when a practice is sold and when a pension owns the building. We claim it once, in the right place.

04 · Fit-outs and leasesDental fit-out capital allowances for squat and leased practices

Fitting out a new (squat) practice, or re-fitting an existing one, is the most common dental claim. A clinical fit-out is packed with qualifying services, and dental fit-out contractors usually document their work well.

You can claim the fit-out you paid for, even on a short lease. Three things can change the claim:

  • If the landlord paid towards the work, that part comes off.
  • The lease wording can affect who is able to claim, so we read it.
  • If you lease from someone connected to you, such as your own pension, the rent should be a market rent set by a surveyor.

Most dental fit-outs fall within the Annual Investment Allowance. That gives full relief in the year of spend, up to a yearly limit that is shared between practices under common control. Above the limit, other first-year reliefs may apply, some for companies only, and the rest is relieved over several years.

For a quick ceiling on a squat fit-out, use the dental capital allowances calculator. Choose D if you lease the premises or C if you own them.

05 · Buying a practiceBuying a dental practice: what tax relief can you claim?

Dental practices change hands often, and dental goodwill can be bought and sold. That means many purchases are share deals. How the deal was set up decides what can be claimed.

How you bought itWhat can usually be claimed
The shares in the practice companyNot a new claim. The company keeps its tax history. If it never claimed on its own fixtures, that claim may still be there. Later fit-out work counts.
The business and its assets, not the buildingGoodwill does not qualify. Fixtures included in the deal, and fit-out work you pay for afterwards, may.
The propertyPart of the price can be for fixtures. What the seller claimed, and any fixed value agreed in the sale, can limit the claim.

Dental practice purchase: the fixtures

When you buy the property, two questions matter most. Did the seller, or an earlier owner, claim on the fixtures? And did you and the seller agree a fixed value for the fixtures in the sale? A low agreed value can cap what you are able to claim. If the seller could not claim, for example a pension scheme or a charity, there may be no earlier claim to limit yours.

The buyer has to show what was bought. The sale contract and the seller's answers to the standard pre-contract questions on capital allowances are the starting point. If you are not sure of any of this, that does not stop the assessment. We find what we can.

Selling a practice?

If you are selling, the fixtures value agreed in the sale affects both sides, and so does the choice between selling shares and selling assets. It is worth talking to us and your accountant before contracts are signed.

06 · Pension ownershipWho claims when a SIPP or SSAS owns the dental premises?

Many dentists hold their surgery building in a SIPP or SSAS pension and let it to their own practice. A pension scheme pays no tax, so it cannot claim capital allowances.

The practice claims its own fit-out instead. How the fixtures were dealt with when the pension bought the building matters, so we ask about it early. We also check:

  • that there is a formal lease at a market rent, set by a surveyor
  • who paid for the fit-out
  • whether the pension, or anyone else connected to you, paid towards it, which reduces the claim

Because you and your pension are connected, values have to be set at market value. We flag these points for specialist review before anything goes on a tax return.

07 · Refurbishing and buildingCapital allowances when you refurbish, extend or build a dental surgery

Practices refurbish surgeries and decontamination rooms often. On a refurbishment, real repairs are split out first. Renewing part of something like for like can be a repair, deducted in full straight away. Stripping out and re-fitting a whole surgery is usually capital spending, and the plant in it can qualify for allowances.

Two points catch practices out:

  • Repairs to run-down premises, done before you start seeing patients, are usually treated as capital, not as repairs.
  • Replacing more than half of a building system, such as the heating or the wiring, is treated as capital, not as a repair.

A new build or extension holds the same surgery systems, but the structure makes up more of the cost, so a smaller share qualifies. The structure itself may get the structures and buildings allowance, a smaller relief spread evenly over many years.

If a practice company converts an older building and removes asbestos or contamination that was there when it bought it, a separate land remediation relief may apply. We flag it where we see it.

08 · TimingCan a dentist claim capital allowances for a fit-out done years ago?

Often, yes. Claims for past dental fit-outs are common and worthwhile. Timing mostly changes how fast the relief comes.

If the tax return for the year of spend can still be amended, the relief can be claimed for that year, and that may bring a refund. If the return can no longer be amended, the allowances can usually still be claimed, but the relief comes more slowly, a share each year.

Before we claim for a past year, we check what has already been claimed, so nothing is claimed twice. The questions the free assessment asks include whether your return is still open.

09 · VAT and grantsVAT, grants and NHS income in a dental capital allowances claim

Dental care is mostly exempt from VAT, so most practices cannot reclaim the VAT on a fit-out. VAT you could not reclaim is part of your real cost and can be included in the claim.

Some practices also do work that is not exempt and reclaim part of their VAT. In that case, we check the share you reclaimed before including any VAT.

Spending paid for by a grant is taken out. Dentistry has no NHS premises grant scheme, and NHS dental contract income is trading income, not a grant, so it does not reduce the claim. That is a key difference from capital allowances for GP surgeries, where premises grants can play a part.

10 · Evidence and costHow a dental practice claim is evidenced, and what it costs

We build the claim from the fit-out contractor's cost breakdown, the mechanical and electrical package and your invoices. Fees and preliminaries are shared across the qualifying items in proportion.

A chartered surveyor values the qualifying items and signs every split of costs. The report shows where the line between loose equipment and fixtures was drawn, and why. Watch-points are listed under where dental claims go wrong.

We work alongside your accountant. You and your accountant get the figures for your tax return and the evidence behind them. If HMRC asks about the claim, we support our valuation and cost split free, with no time limit. This does not include representation at a tax tribunal.

Our fee is a flat 3% of the capital allowances we identify. The free assessment costs nothing, and if there is no claim, we tell you early, in writing. We work with practices in England and Wales. Questions

Questions from dental practices

Something not covered here? Talk to a person about your practice.

Can I claim capital allowances on a dental surgery fit-out?

Often, yes. Fitting out a new (squat) practice or refurbishing one is the most common dental claim. The suction and air pipe runs, the services at each chair and the decontamination room can qualify. These built-in services are often the part that has never been claimed.

My accountant has already claimed our equipment. Is anything left?

There may be. Accountants usually claim the loose equipment, such as chairs, X-ray machines and computers. The services built into the building are often missed. We check your fixed asset register first, so nothing is claimed twice.

Can dentists claim capital allowances on the surgery building?

On the parts that count as plant, yes. The shell, the land and goodwill do not qualify. The systems built into it, such as heating, lighting, water, suction and compressed air, can. A dental practice is a trade, so the usual limit on homes does not apply, apart from any flat above.

I bought a dental practice. Can I claim?

It depends on how you bought it. If you bought the shares in the practice company, the purchase itself is not a new claim, but the company may still have an unclaimed fixtures claim of its own. If you bought the property, part of the price can be for qualifying fixtures. Goodwill does not qualify.

My pension scheme owns the surgery building. Who can claim?

A SIPP or SSAS pays no tax, so it cannot claim. The practice can claim the fit-out it paid for. How the fixtures were dealt with when the pension bought the building matters, so we ask about it early.

Can I claim capital allowances if I lease my dental surgery?

Usually, yes. A practice can claim the fit-out it paid for, even on a short lease. If the landlord paid part of the cost, that part comes off. The lease wording can matter, so we read it.

Is the dental chair a fixture or equipment?

It is plant either way, but whether it is a fixture or loose equipment is a judgement. Many accountants already claim it as equipment. We check your records and claim it once, in the right place.

Can I claim for a dental fit-out done several years ago?

Often, yes. If the tax return for that year can still be amended, relief can come sooner and may bring a refund. If it cannot, the relief can usually still come, but more slowly. We check which applies and what has already been claimed.

Does NHS dental contract income reduce the claim?

No. NHS dental contract income is trading income, not a grant, so it does not reduce what you can claim. Dentistry has no NHS premises grant scheme. If any of your spending was paid for by a grant, that part is taken out.

Our fee is a flat 3% of the capital allowances we identify. The free assessment costs nothing. If there is no claim, we tell you early, in writing.
