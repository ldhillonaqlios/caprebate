In brief

Can a care home claim capital allowances?

The short answer

Usually, yes. Capital allowances for care homes give tax relief on the fixtures inside the building, such as nurse call, lifts, heating, hoists and fire alarms. The business must pay UK income or corporation tax. How much it can claim depends on how residents live, who owns and runs the home, and what any previous owner claimed.

The walls, roof and land are not fixtures. A chartered surveyor values the qualifying items and signs the split of costs, and your accountant puts the figures in your tax return.

Key points

  • Residential care homes, nursing homes and dementia units can all qualify.
  • Rooms in a registered care setting usually open a fuller claim than self-contained flats.
  • Buying, building, extending, refurbishing and fitting out can each lead to a claim.
  • On a purchase, what the seller claimed and any fixtures value agreed in the sale matter most.
  • Charities, pension funds and businesses on cash basis accounts cannot usually claim.
  • The assessment is free. Our fee is a flat 3% of the allowances we identify.

What decides a claim

What decides a care home claim

"Care home" covers everything from a nursing home to extra-care flats, and the tax rules treat them differently.

General notes · 1 to 4

  1. 01

    How residents live

    Rooms in a registered care setting: a fuller claim is usually open. Self-contained flats let by an investor: only shared, staff and care areas, with their costs identified separately. Tenants in their own homes: that is supported living.

  2. 02

    Who owns it and who runs it

    If one company owns the building and a connected company runs the home, the operator needs a formal lease. Without one, its claim is weaker and a tax charge can arise.

  3. 03

    Sale and leaseback

    Common with care homes, and a sale for tax. It can mean a tax charge for the seller, so a specialist handles it.

  4. 04

    What the seller claimed

    A previous claim, a low agreed value or a past transfer between connected companies can shrink a claim, sometimes to nil.

General notes · 5 to 8

  1. 05

    Grants

    Grant-funded spending cannot also be claimed, so we set it aside.

  2. 06

    VAT you cannot reclaim

    Care homes are largely VAT-exempt. VAT you cannot reclaim can count towards the claim.

  3. 07

    Claimed once only

    Fixtures can be claimed once. A past claim on loose furniture alone does not use them up.

  4. 08

    Who can claim, and when

    The business must pay UK income or corporation tax and not use cash basis accounts. If a return can no longer be amended, relief is slower, not lost.

01 · The basicsWhat are capital allowances for care homes?

Capital allowances are a tax relief. They let a business take part of what it spent on equipment and fixtures off its taxable profits. For a care home, that means the systems built into the building that let it work as a care home, not the bricks and mortar.

They reduce income tax or corporation tax. They are not a capital gains tax relief.

When a home is bought or built, the price is usually one figure. It does not say how much went on the nurse call, the lifts or the heating. Finding and valuing those items is the job of a care home capital allowances claim.

Residential care homes, nursing homes and dementia units are all in scope. Children's residential homes are checked case by case. Where residents are tenants in their own homes, the rules are different: see our page on capital allowances for supported living.

02 · What qualifiesWhich care home fixtures qualify?

The test is what each item does in your care business. So a claim is built item by item, not copied from a list. These systems usually make up most of a care home claim:

  • nurse call and emergency call systems
  • passenger, through-floor and platform lifts, and stairlifts
  • heating, hot and cold water, and ventilation
  • the electrics, lighting and emergency lighting
  • fire alarms, detection and sprinklers
  • ceiling and fixed hoists, assisted baths and wet rooms
  • sluice rooms, bedpan washers, the commercial laundry and the catering kitchen
  • dementia-safe door access, CCTV, intruder alarms and a standby generator

We also look at sanitaryware, data cabling, signage, fitted furniture, floor coverings and handrails. Building work done to put a system in can count with that system. Examples are the opening formed for a through-floor lift, or the base for a standby generator.

Worth knowing

Some building systems, such as the wiring, lighting, heating, water and passenger lifts, count whatever they do. Others, such as hoists and nurse call, must pass a test on what they do in your home. The surveyor records that test for each item.

03 · How residents liveResidential care home, nursing home or extra care: does it matter?

Yes. It is the biggest single question in a care home claim, because the tax rules restrict claims on dwellings.

In a registered care home, residents have rooms in a care setting, with shared lounges and dining, and personal or nursing care is provided. The home is run as a care business, not as a set of dwellings, so a fuller claim is usually open. Registration with CQC in England or Care Inspectorate Wales helps show this.

Extra-care and assisted-living schemes are different. Where residents have self-contained flats with their own kitchen and bathroom, and an investor lets them, the flats count as dwellings. Only the shared, staff and care areas can qualify, and their costs must be identified separately. A claim can fail if that split is not made.

04 · Who can claimWho can claim care home capital allowances?

The business must pay UK income tax or corporation tax on its profits. Sole traders, partnerships, LLPs and limited companies can all claim. Some of the fastest reliefs, such as full expensing, are for limited companies only. The annual investment allowance and writing-down allowances are open to all of them.

Charities, pension funds and NHS bodies do not usually pay this tax, so they cannot usually claim. A business that uses cash basis accounts cannot claim on property fixtures either. If it moves to normal (accruals) accounts, a claim may open up.

When one company owns the home and another runs it

Many care groups split ownership. One company owns the building and a connected company runs the home. The operator can claim on the fit-out it paid for if it has a formal written lease. With only a licence or an informal arrangement, its claim is weaker. Spending by the operator on a building it does not own can also cause a tax charge, and whether the two companies are in the same group matters. A tax specialist reviews the lease before any claim is made.

Investors and landlords

An investor who owns a care home and lets it to an operator can claim on the qualifying fixtures it owns. If the landlord paid towards the operator's fit-out, that part comes off the operator's claim. The landlord may be able to claim on its contribution instead.

05 · Buying a care homeCan you claim capital allowances when buying a care home?

Often, yes. Part of the price you paid can be for fixtures. What you can claim on a care home purchase depends mostly on the seller's history.

How the seller's history changes a claim on a care home purchase
The sellerWhat it usually means for you
Claimed on the fixturesYour claim usually rests on a fixtures value agreed with the seller. A low value can cap it
Could have claimed, but the value was not agreed properlyYour claim on those fixtures can be nil

A past transfer between connected companies can also limit what can be claimed, so we check the ownership chain.

The capital allowances election

A capital allowances election is a joint document between buyer and seller. It fixes the value of the fixtures passing to you. It must cover fixtures only and be made within a strict time limit. One that includes loose equipment can be invalid. If you have one, it is one of the first things we read. Building work you pay for yourself after the purchase is claimed separately.

The replies to the purchase enquiries often record the capital allowances position too. A blank or "not applicable" answer does not close the door. It is a prompt to look further. It is up to the buyer to show what can be claimed, so the paperwork matters.

Buying the company, not the property

If you bought the shares in the company that owns the home, the purchase itself is not a new claim. The company keeps the tax position it already had. Building work or a fit-out done since can still count.

Sale and leaseback, and selling a home

Sale and leaseback is common with care homes. For tax, it is a sale. It brings in a fixtures election and can mean a tax charge for the seller, so we pass it to a specialist. If you are selling a care home, a review before the sale can help protect you from an unexpected charge.

06 · Building or extendingCapital allowances on a new care home or an extension

When you build or extend a care home, the cost of the qualifying systems can be claimed. A build cost breakdown, or a breakdown of the mechanical and electrical costs, makes the split faster. Without one, the surveyor values the items instead.

Building work done to put plant in place goes with that plant. In a care home, that can include lift openings, trunking for nurse call, and drainage for sluice rooms and assisted bathrooms.

The structure itself is not plant. A separate structures and buildings allowance may apply to building work done on or after 29 October 2018. Dwellings are barred from it, but a registered care home that provides personal care may fall within an exception. Self-contained extra-care flats do not. This area is not settled, so a specialist checks it for your home. Unlike allowances on fixtures, this allowance can affect the tax on a later sale, so your accountant weighs it up.

07 · RefurbishmentCare home refurbishment: repairs, improvements and tax relief

A refurbishment is often the richest claim. Rewiring, a new heating system, new assisted bathrooms or a new nurse call system can all hold qualifying spend.

Part of a refurbishment may be repairs rather than improvements. Genuine like-for-like repairs can often be deducted in full straight away, which is faster than capital allowances. The line between a repair and an improvement is a judgement, and HMRC checks it, so we record why each item sits where it does.

There is one trap. If you bought a home in a run-down state and repaired it before you used it, those repairs usually count as capital, not repairs.

Worth knowing

If the work involved removing asbestos or dealing with contamination you did not cause, a limited company may be able to claim an extra deduction called land remediation relief. We flag it when it applies.

08 · Grants and VATGrants, VAT and other costs in a care home claim

Spending paid for by a grant cannot also be claimed, so we set that part aside. If you are not sure whether a grant was involved, we check rather than guess.

Care homes are largely VAT-exempt, so many cannot reclaim the VAT they pay. VAT you cannot reclaim is part of your real cost, so it can count towards the claim. On a purchase, stamp duty land tax and fees can count too. If the business has a mix of exempt and other income, the VAT position needs checking first.

09 · TimingHow far back can a care home capital allowances claim go?

It depends on your tax returns, not only on when you spent the money. If the return for that year can still be amended, faster reliefs and even a refund may be possible. If it can no longer be amended, the relief is not lost. It comes through more slowly in later years.

So a home bought or refurbished some years ago can still be worth checking. Fixtures can only be claimed once, though. If you or your accountant have already claimed on them, there is nothing more to claim on those items. A past claim on loose furniture and equipment alone does not use up the fixtures.

10 · Evidence and costHow a care home claim is evidenced, and what it costs

A claim is only as strong as its evidence. A chartered surveyor values the qualifying items and signs the split of costs. On a purchase, that split shares the price between the fixtures and the rest of the property. The report records what each item does in your care business and why it qualifies.

We work alongside your accountant, who puts the figures in your tax return. If HMRC asks questions, we support our valuation and cost split free, with no time limit. That does not include tribunal representation.

The assessment is free. If it is worth going on, our fee is a flat 3% of the capital allowances we identify. If there is no claim, we tell you early, in writing. Questions

Questions about care home capital allowances

Something not covered here? Talk to a person about your home.

Does a care home count as a dwelling for capital allowances?

It depends on how residents live and who runs the home. A registered care home with rooms, shared lounges and care is usually treated as a care business, so a fuller claim is usually open. Where an investor lets self-contained flats, only the shared, staff and care areas can qualify.

Do nurse call systems and patient hoists qualify?

They can. They are equipment of the care business, so the test is what each item does in your home. The surveyor records that for each item, with the evidence.

We bought our care home. Can we still claim?

Often, yes. It depends on whether a previous owner claimed, whether a value for the fixtures was agreed in the sale, and who the seller was. A developer or charity seller may leave a fresh claim.

One company owns our home and a connected company runs it. Who claims?

It depends on the lease. With a formal written lease, the operating company can claim on the fit-out it paid for. With only a licence, its claim is weaker and a tax charge can arise, so a tax specialist reviews the lease first.

Our home is VAT-exempt. Does the VAT we paid count?

VAT you cannot reclaim is part of your real cost, so it can count towards the claim. If the business has a mix of exempt and other income, that needs checking first.

Can a nursing home claim capital allowances?

Usually, yes. A nursing home or dementia unit is run as a care business, and it can hold more qualifying equipment than a standard residential care home, such as hoists, assisted baths and sluice rooms. The same tests apply: the business must pay UK tax, and who owns and runs the home matters.

We bought our care home years ago. Is it too late to claim?

Not always. If the tax return for that year can still be amended, relief can come faster and may mean a refund. If not, the relief is not lost. It comes through more slowly in later years. Fixtures can only be claimed once, so we check what has been claimed already.

What is a capital allowances election on a care home purchase?

It is a joint document between buyer and seller that fixes the value of the fixtures passing to the buyer. A low value can cap your claim, and one that wrongly includes loose equipment can be invalid. If you have one, send it to us with the sale contract.

Can a charity or pension fund claim capital allowances on a care home?

Not usually, because they do not pay the tax that capital allowances reduce. Two things are still worth knowing. A review before you sell can protect you from an unexpected tax charge, and a trading tenant in your building may have a claim of its own.

What does a care home capital allowances claim cost?

The assessment is free. If it is worth going on, our fee is a flat 3% of the capital allowances we identify. If HMRC asks, we support our valuation and cost split free, with no time limit. That does not include tribunal representation.
